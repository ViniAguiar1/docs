---
title: "What Keep is"
description: "Crosses your recurring revenue with product usage and pings you on Slack about who is leaving, ordered by how much money is at stake."
---

Churn is decided weeks before you find out. By the time the cancellation email
lands, the decision was made and the conversation happened without you.

Keep connects your billing to your product usage, shows which paying accounts
have stopped showing up, and sends the alert on Slack.

## Where the revenue comes from

  </Card>
  <Card title="Asaas" icon="building-columns">
    A read-only key, pasted in **Integrations**. Boleto, Pix, or card — the
    subscriptions land in the same MRR and the same risk list.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

All three add up. Whoever charges through more than one sees a single MRR.

## Order matters more than the signal

The list comes ordered by **how much money is at stake**, not by who dropped the most.

A 90% drop on a R$90 account can wait. A 40% drop on a R$4,200 account cannot.
Nearly every churn tool orders by signal severity, which looks good on a chart
and is useless on a Monday morning, when you have time to call three people and
need to know which three.

## Where the usage comes from

Two sources, and they add up.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="PostHog" icon="chart-line">
    Connects over OAuth, read-only. Keep matches each customer by email.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Your own ingestion" icon="code" href="/quickstart">
    One HTTP call from your backend. It is the only way the match is exact,
    because you send the customer id instead of letting us guess by email.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Note>
  In B2B, whoever pays the invoice is rarely who uses the product: billing goes
  to `finance@` and access comes from someone else. That is why email matching
  has a ceiling, and why sending the `customerId` changes the nature of the
  problem instead of improving the heuristic.
</Note>
